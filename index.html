I was an education researcher in the School of Education at UNSW Sydney and Vice President for Operations & Research at LifeCollege in Puerto Princesa, Palawan, where I previously served as School Principal. My work sits between quantitative education research and the practical business of running and improving a school.

My research examines teachers’ digital and AI competence (through the TPACK framework), data literacy, and educational measurement, using large-scale international assessments — PISA, TIMSS, ICILS, and ICCS. This extends into an ongoing research programme on digital inequality and the socioeconomic achievement gap, which asks how unequal access to digital resources — and the unequal capacity to convert them into learning — is produced and sustained. I frame this with the Capability Approach and Digital Capital Theory, treating schools and opportunities to learn as institutional conversion factors rather than neutral inputs.

Methodologically I work in R and STATA: structural equation modelling, measurement-invariance testing across many groups, and IRT graded-response modelling, with a consistent concern for what the evidence can and cannot be claimed to show. At LifeCollege I lead enrolment strategy, assessment-focused teacher development, and a knowledge-rich, formation-oriented instructional framework.

Publications

Measurement Invariance of Adolescents’ Digital Citizenship: Evidence from ICCS 2016 and 2022 In preparation

Abril, J. M. V.

Prepared for the AERA 2027 Annual Meeting, Toronto (working title)

Summary

Examines the measurement structure, cross-group invariance, and latent positioning of adolescents’ digital citizenship using ICCS 2016 (81,707 students, 15 countries) and ICCS 2022 (33,664 students, 13 countries). Six latent constructs are modelled with confirmatory and multi-group CFA on ordinal indicators (WLSMV, theta parameterisation); invariance is tested across gender, immigrant background, and country, with alignment optimisation as a sensitivity check. The Capability Approach supplies the equity rationale for treating invariance as a precondition for fair comparison.

From Digital Capabilities to Digital Civic Engagement: Opportunities to Learn Safe and Responsible Digital Conduct across ICILS 2018 and ICILS 2023 Accepted

Abril, J. M. V.

BERA 2026 Annual Conference, Manchester — individual paper

Summary

Frames opportunities to learn as institutional conversion factors within the Capability Approach and models digital capabilities and digital civic engagement across ICILS 2018 (N = 44,799) and ICILS 2023 (N = 135,615). A three-factor structure in 2018 and a five-factor structure in 2023 fit well, but digital civic engagement shows near-zero associations with opportunity-to-learn predictors — complicating the assumption that formal instruction translates straightforwardly into online civic participation.

Digital Resources, School Quality, and the Socioeconomic Achievement Gap: A Structural Equation Analysis of TIMSS 2023 In progress

Abril, J. M. V.

Working paper in progress (working title)

Summary

Uses TIMSS 2023 Grade 4 data and achievement plausible values to specify competing structural equation models (in lavaan) linking digital resources, digital competence, school quality, and conversion factors to socioeconomic gaps in achievement. Pooled and region-specific models are compared not only on fit but on the direction, magnitude, and ordering of standardised pathways, within a Capability Approach and Digital Capital framing.

Teachers’ Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge: Cross-Country Evidence from PISA 2018 and 2022 Under revision

Abril, J. M. V.

Manuscript under revision (working title)

Summary

Investigates what follows when selected PISA indicators are treated as theoretically informed proxies for TPACK-related constructs, rather than as independently validated scales. The framing distinguishes exploratory secondary analysis from formal construct validation and states the epistemic position explicitly: the indicators support interpretation as proxies while their status as validated TPACK, TPK, or TK scales remains a stated limitation.
